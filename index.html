About

Rigorous science, built for real decisions.

Since joining the University of Notre Dame, I have developed a research program on understanding and managing water-related risks in human-impacted watersheds. My work connects process-based hydrologic modeling, geospatial analysis, climate information, machine learning and optimization.

The goal is science that is credible and useful: tools that communities, watershed organizations, engineers and conservation professionals can actually use to compare options under uncertainty.

I hold a Ph.D. in Agricultural and Biological Engineering from Purdue University (advisor Bernard Engel), an M.E. in Geo-detection and Information Technology from China University of Geosciences, Beijing, and a B.S. in Geosciences from Suez Canal University, Egypt. Before my current role I was a postdoctoral associate at Notre Dame, working with Diogo Bolster and Jennifer Tank.

Research

Three connected threads

A progression from understanding hydrologic processes, to evaluating interventions, to helping stakeholders compare choices under uncertainty.

Watershed response to climate & management

SWAT models of the St. Joseph River Basin quantify how land-use change, future climate and winter cover crops shift river flow, sediment, nitrate and phosphorus delivery to Lake Michigan.

SWATGroundwater–surface waterCover crops

Probabilistic urban climate risk

Flood-exposure and water-security indicators for cities worldwide, and an equity-centered probabilistic flood-risk framework for Indianapolis that shows how risk may shift and who bears it.

ERA5-LandGoogle Earth EngineVulnerability

Accelerated decision support

Automated workflows linking process-based models with machine-learning emulators, large scenario ensembles, uncertainty analysis and multi-objective optimization, so tradeoffs can be explored in minutes, not months.

ML emulatorsOptimizationCLEAR Watersheds

Where this is heading

  • Demonstration: moving CLEAR Watersheds from framework to application, and testing whether fast emulators preserve hydrologically important behavior.
  • Interventions in combination: cover crops, upstream storage, drainage management, natural infrastructure and stormwater, evaluated together across rural, urban and basin scales.
  • Uncertainty that matters: separating what better data can reduce from what robust planning must absorb, with social vulnerability built in.

Projects

Selected work

2025 – now

CLEAR Watersheds

Community-Led Environmental AI for Resilient Watersheds

Automated, hydrologically sound modeling workflows that pair process-based models with ML emulators and optimization, built to give flood-, water-quality- and agriculture-minded partners a shared view of tradeoffs across rural–urban landscapes.

2025 – now

Elkhart River Watershed · Clean Water Indiana

with Elkhart County Soil & Water Conservation District

A watershed model and scenario framework for sediment, nutrients, conservation practices and water-budget questions, shaped by local priorities and field-monitoring input. A practical testbed for CLEAR methods.

2025 – now

Community-Centered Optimization for Rural–Urban Water Systems

Just Transformations to Sustainability Initiative

Stochastic hydrologic modeling and multi-objective optimization to weigh flood reduction, runoff management, water quality and agricultural productivity.

2024 – 2026

Mapping Tomorrow’s Flood · Indianapolis

Probabilistic, equity-centered flood risk

Integrates climate projections, urban development, exposure and socioeconomic vulnerability to show how flood risk may shift spatially and affect vulnerable communities. Published in Natural Hazards.

2023 – 2024

Global Urban Climate Assessment (GUCA)

Public platform for equitable climate-resilience planning

Flood-exposure and water-security indicators from ERA5-Land and Google Earth Engine across cities in very different climatic and socioeconomic settings.

2022 – 2024

Rural–Urban Interface · St. Joseph River Basin

Groundwater–surface water, land use and cover crops

Two SWAT studies on historical land-use change and on winter cover crops under future climate, plus a Pine Creek watershed nutrient-runoff model.
